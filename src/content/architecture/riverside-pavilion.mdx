## Concept

The Riverside Pavilion sits at the edge of a public park where the grass gives way to a rocky riverbank. The brief called for a structure that could host small outdoor events — readings, markets, informal gatherings — without dominating the landscape.

The design response was deliberate restraint. A simple timber portal frame, open on all four sides, raised slightly on concrete piers to acknowledge the flood plain. The roof is a shallow monopitch with a deep overhang on the river-facing side, creating a threshold condition: sheltered from above, open to the water and sky.

## Materials

- Primary structure: glulam timber portal frames at 3m centres
- Roof: corrugated Colorbond steel on timber purlins
- Floor: recycled hardwood decking on steel sub-frame
- Piers: in-situ concrete, finished with bush-hammered texture

## What I was Thinking About

I was interested in the difference between a *building* and a *shelter*. A building closes the world out. A shelter negotiates. The pavilion is about that negotiation — giving just enough enclosure to make people comfortable, while keeping the river and the trees and the sky as the real architecture.
