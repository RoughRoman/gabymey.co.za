Before the birds decide to speak,
the light arrives on softer feet —
a yellow hush across the floor,
a held breath just inside the door.

I watch it stretch along the wall
and think of nothing much at all,
which is, I've found, the closest thing
to peace that any morning brings.

Outside, the street begins to stir.
Inside, the kettle starts to purr.
The light moves on. The day is made.
I write this down before it fades.
